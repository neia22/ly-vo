How to screen tenants in Namibia: a 2026 landlord's guide

A good tenant pays on time, looks after your property and stays. A bad one can cost you months of lost rent. Here's how to tell them apart — fairly, and without scaring off the good ones.

L Lyvo · 3 June 2026 · 8 min read

In most Namibian rental markets, screening still happens on instinct — a phone call, a gut feel, a handshake. That works until it doesn't. A structured process protects your income without adding friction. Here are the five checks that matter, in order.

1. Verify identity

Start with a valid Namibian ID (or passport for non-citizens). Confirm the name matches everything else the applicant gives you, and that the photo matches the person. Verifying a phone number and email at this stage also cuts out time-wasters and gives you a reliable way to reach them.

2. Confirm income and affordability

The single best predictor of on-time rent is whether the tenant can comfortably afford it. A common rule of thumb is that rent should be no more than about 30% of gross monthly income — once you're above 40–50%, late payments become far more likely.

Ask for proof: recent payslips for salaried tenants, or 3–6 months of bank or mobile-money statements for the self-employed and informally employed. In Namibia, plenty of reliable tenants earn through trade or informal work — statements let you assess them fairly instead of turning them away.

3. Take references

Two references tell you most of what you need:

  • The previous landlord — did they pay on time, look after the place, and give proper notice? (Ask the landlord before the current one, who may just want them gone.)
  • An employer or a guarantor — confirms income stability, or gives you someone to fall back on.

4. Assess prior rental behaviour

Past behaviour predicts future behaviour. Look for a track record of completed tenancies, consistent payments and no history of repeated defaults or evictions. A tenant who can show a clean rental history is worth a lot — and a tenant building one for the first time is worth giving a fair chance with a guarantor.

5. Watch the red flags

Most rental disasters are visible in the application — if you know what to look for.
  • Reluctance to provide ID, statements or references.
  • Income that doesn't add up, or rent above a healthy share of it.
  • Pressure to skip checks or pay cash with "no paperwork".
  • Conflicting information between documents.

Keep it fair — and fast

Screening shouldn't punish good applicants. Use a consistent checklist for everyone (it also keeps you on the right side of fair-housing practice), ask only for what you need, and turn applications around quickly. The best tenants have options — a clear, fast, respectful process is itself a competitive advantage.

Key takeaways

  • Verify ID, then income — affordability is the #1 predictor of on-time rent.
  • Accept bank/mobile-money statements so you can fairly assess informal income.
  • Always call the previous landlord, not just the current one.
  • Use the same checklist for every applicant, and decide quickly.
